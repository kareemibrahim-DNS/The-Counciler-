# Line items, list prices and quote structure

## Where the numbers come from
- Every partner's line items and **list (selling) prices** are in references/catalog.md. Source: Salesforce Standard Price Book (SAR, AED, KWD, JOD, EGP) and "USD 1 – International", refreshed 14 Nov 2026. 29 partners, about 200 line items.
- Always take a price from references/catalog.md. Never quote a price from memory, from another country, or from an old sheet.
- Use the **exact Salesforce line-item name** in every quote.
- A line with no price in a currency is **not sellable in that country**. Say so; do not convert currencies.
- Lines marked ⚠ have inconsistent prices across currencies: tell the person to verify in Salesforce before quoting.
- The catalogue holds list prices only. Cost, markup and margin are locked and are never in the catalogue.

## Country → currency
KSA → SAR · UAE → AED · Kuwait → KWD · Jordan → JOD · Egypt → EGP · anywhere else → USD (International price book).

## One country per answer
- Show prices for **one country only**: the one named in the question; otherwise the asker's own country if you know it; otherwise ask "Which country is this for (KSA, UAE, Kuwait, Jordan, Egypt or International)?" before quoting.
- Never list every country's price unless the person asks for all countries or a comparison across countries.

## How to build a quote
1. Get: partner, country, number of branches, billing frequency, deal type (New Business / Expansion / Renewal / Upgrade-Downgrade / Re-activation).
2. Find the partner in references/catalog.md and use the country's column. Follow that partner's quote rules exactly.
3. Show: Line item | Type | Qty | Unit list price | Line total, then the total, with the arithmetic.
4. Run the structure checks below.
5. If the merchant asks for a discount, don't promise or estimate one. Point to the discount flow in 12-working-with-resellers.

## Structure checks (most common mistakes)
- **Main + Additional:** most partners are 1 Main license + (branches − 1) Additional Branch lines of the same plan (BrandWallet, ShopBrain, FlowERP, Prepit, Ordable, Xero, QuickBooks, Alpha). License count must equal branch count.
- **Branches included in the plan:** Wafeq Premium = 1 branch only; Wafeq Enterprise includes 3 (Additional Branch only on Enterprise). Zyda Basic includes 1, Zyda Advanced includes 3.
- **Minimums:** Supy needs at least 3 licenses, plus a one-time Onboarding Fee on new business.
- **Per account, not per branch:** InvoiceQ and Iisal. InvoiceQ Basic covers up to 2 terminals; InvoiceQ Advanced is per issuing terminal. Iisal tier is chosen by yearly invoice volume.
- **Virtual brands:** Grubtech = Virtual Brand add-on per extra brand; Deliverect = Virtual License per virtual branch.
- **Usage lines:** OptCulture SMS and WhatsApp are billed per message on top of the license. Tell the merchant.
- **Only with SAP:** Malachite cannot be sold without SAP.
- **Inactive:** Nitx is deactivated. Never quote it.

## Telling partners in the same category apart (and who fits this merchant)
Use the category section in references/catalog.md for a side-by-side (best for, edge, licensing unit, countries, entry price, watch-outs, how to choose). Be precise about the differences. When someone describes a merchant or a business case, recommend the partner that fits it best and say why, in one line, labelled "Recommendation". Examples:
- Ordering platforms: Grubtech (multi-brand, kitchen + dispatch) · Deliverect (menu control and analytics, highest price) · UrbanPiper (lowest entry, only option for Egypt) · FeedUs (KSA local aggregators only).
- Inventory: ShopBrain (tiered per branch, mid price, optional accounting) · Supy (premium per outlet, 3-license minimum, deepest procurement and recipe costing).

# Working with reseller partners (for every Foodics colleague)

## Centralized Communication rule
- Other Foodics teams must **not** talk to reseller partners directly. Breaking this is a violation.
- Everything goes through Partnerships: one point of contact and one email channel per case.

## Unified billing
- Any deal, started by Foodics or by the partner, is invoiced and collected by Foodics. The partner focuses on closing deals, and the merchant deals with one bill from Foodics.

## Unified pricing
- The list price of a service is the same whether Foodics or the partner sells it, in every country. So there is no price burning between Foodics and the partner when both talk to the same merchant.
- If a partner isn't on unified pricing, Foodics' list price is still lower than the partner's own price.

## How to sell a reseller partner
1. Pick the partner that fits the merchant (the category section in references/catalog.md shows the side-by-side).
2. Build the quote from the list prices and the partner's quote structure.
3. Submit the deal or lead with that partner's submission form (see references/catalog.md). Partnerships takes it from there.
- **UrbanPiper:** unified billing is still being set up. Its form is for lead referrals only: send closed UrbanPiper deals to Partnerships in #reseller-hub.

## Requests: use the flow, don't improvise
All requests go to **#reseller-hub** on Slack, one Workflow Builder form per request type:
- **Discount** → the "i need discount" form. Include: F5, brand, branch count, discount %, scope (one or several F5) and the expansion plan.
- **Refund** → the "i need refund" form.
- **Cancellation** → the "i need cancellation" form.
- **Anything else** (a question or an exception) → post it in #reseller-hub.
Partnerships reviews and approves every request and replies in the thread. Never promise a discount, refund or exception to a merchant before it is approved.

## Renewals
- If a merchant won't renew, the account owner must tell Partnerships **before the renewal date**. The Salesforce alert is only a signal, not the flag.

## Blocked accounts
- MP block requests come only through **#mp-block-requests**.

# Reseller Program cycles at a glance (generic level)

Use this for a generic answer: the cycle exists, where an issue usually comes from, and what to take care of. For deeper detail (exact dates, grace periods, rules, why a cycle works this way), ask Kareem Ibrahim or Hatem Ashour.

## Request cycle (#reseller-hub)
- **How it runs:** request form in #reseller-hub, Partnerships reviews, a decision is posted in the thread.
- **Issues usually come from:** missing details (F5, brand, branch count, scope), or something promised to the merchant before approval.
- **Take care:** fill in every field, and promise nothing until it is approved.

## Billing cycle (unified billing)
- **How it runs:** Foodics invoices and collects every reseller deal. Dates and change windows follow the Reseller Policy.
- **Issues usually come from:** promising the merchant a date or a change before Partnerships confirms it.
- **Take care:** check with Partnerships before you promise a refund, cancellation, upgrade or downgrade.

## Renewal cycle
- **How it runs:** renewal invoices go out ahead of the renewal date. Payment terms and renewal pricing follow the Reseller Policy.
- **Issues usually come from:** a non-renewal flagged too late, or a renewal price promised before checking with Partnerships.
- **Take care:** tell Partnerships about a non-renewal **before** the renewal date. The Salesforce alert alone is not the flag.

## Bought but not live
- **How it runs:** a service that was bought but isn't live yet goes to Partnerships, who decide how billing is handled.
- **Issues usually come from:** promising the merchant a refund or a fix before Partnerships decides.
- **Take care:** raise it with Partnerships in #reseller-hub.

## Blocked accounts (MP block)
- **How it runs:** a blocked account goes from #mp-block-requests to the partner, who deactivates the service.
- **Issues usually come from:** a request sent outside #mp-block-requests.
- **Take care:** use #mp-block-requests only.

## Partner tickets
- **How it runs:** a partner ticket stays with Partnerships until it is closed.
- **Issues usually come from:** a ticket stuck because one side is waiting on the other.
- **Take care:** keep the case in one thread with Partnerships, and never contact the partner directly.

# Competitive analysis

Use this when someone asks who fits a merchant best, or brings something to compare: a competitor's offer, a partner's own quote, a screenshot, a pasted price list or a file.

## Which country
- Use the **merchant's** country when the question says where the merchant is (a city counts: Riyadh → KSA, Dubai → UAE, Kuwait City → Kuwait, Amman → Jordan, Cairo → Egypt). Otherwise use the asker's country. If it isn't clear, ask.
- Outside the five markets (for example Bahrain, Qatar, Oman): use the USD (International) prices only.
- A line with no price in that currency is not sold there. If a partner lists the country but has no price there, say so and don't recommend it there until Partnerships confirms in #reseller-hub.

## Who fits this merchant
1. Get what you need, and ask only for what is missing: country, number of branches, number of brands, the sales channels or aggregators they use, and the main pain (for example menu sync, stock, loyalty, accounting).
2. Use the category section in references/catalog.md for that country. Pick on the category's "How to choose" first, then compare best for, edge, licensing unit, countries, price and watch-outs.
3. Answer with:
   - **Recommendation:** one partner, in one line, and why it fits this merchant.
   - **The math:** that partner's full quote for the merchant's size (lines × quantities = total), not just the entry price.
   - **When the others win:** one line each, for the other partners in the category.
4. Several needs (for example ordering and loyalty): one Recommendation per need, each from its own category. Two partners that fit equally: say it's close and name the one question that decides it.
5. If no live partner sells in that country, or none fits, say so plainly. Never stretch a partner to fit.

## Comparing an offer someone brings
1. **Read it.** Use the pasted text, or read the attached file. Use only what the offer says. If something is not in it, write "not in their offer". Never guess a competitor's price, feature or billing frequency. If a screenshot or file is unclear or cut off, list what you could read and ask for the rest. If their offer has no prices, compare scope and features only.
2. **Only a competitor's name, no offer:** the Reselling Brain holds no competitor data. Don't describe their product or prices from memory. Ask for their offer, and meanwhile give our side: the partner that fits and its price for the merchant's size.
3. **Find our match** in references/catalog.md: the partner or partners that solve the same need in that country.
4. **Make it like for like:**
   - **Same billing period.** Use our line for the same period when the partner has one (monthly against monthly, annual against yearly). If our partner is sold only annually, turn their monthly price into a full-year total and say ours is billed annually.
   - **Same scope:** branches, brands, modules. If the catalogue doesn't say whether one line covers all branches, say so and ask to confirm in #reseller-hub before treating the prices as equal.
   - **Different licensing units** (per user, per device or per order against our per branch or per account): compare totals for the merchant's real size, never unit price against unit price.
   - **One-time fees stay apart** from recurring fees, on both sides. The headline and the Recommendation compare recurring fees only. A first-year total that adds the one-time fees can be an extra line, never the headline.
   - **Same currency only.** If their offer is in another currency, show both sides in their own currency, side by side. Never convert either side and don't say which is cheaper.
   - If their offer shows a price cut or free months, compare what the merchant pays over a full year using only what the offer states.
5. **Show a table:** item | their offer | our match (exact Salesforce line name) | note. Then the totals and the arithmetic.
6. **Say what differs**, factually, without guessing what a partner covers when the catalogue doesn't say (for example which aggregators); mark it to confirm in #reseller-hub: features, licensing unit, minimums, what is included and what is extra. Something in their offer we don't match: write "no match in our catalogue". A feature the catalogue doesn't mention: write "not in the Reselling Brain", not "our partner doesn't have it".
7. **What a reseller partner through Foodics adds,** from 12-working-with-resellers:
   - unified billing: one bill from Foodics;
   - unified pricing: the list price is the same whether Foodics or the partner sells it, so there is no price burning;
   - one point of contact: Partnerships handles the partner.
8. **Close with a Recommendation** (which option fits this merchant, and why) and the next step: build the quote, then submit it with that partner's form.

## When the asker wants something to send the merchant

- Keep the comparison table, the totals, what's included, and what buying through Foodics adds.
- Leave out watch-outs, "When the others win", partners you didn't recommend, Partnerships' internal flows and channels, and the words "price burning".

- An offer from a company we don't resell can't be quoted, billed or submitted through us. If the asker wants Foodics to resell it, that's new partner outreach: name Kareem Ibrahim (Hatem Ashour when Kareem is away).
- Never share cost, margins, discounts or partner terms. Stay factual about competitors; never run them down.
- Never contact the competitor or the partner, and never tell a colleague to (Centralized Communication rule).
